# Delivery Studio user manual

This manual explains the local planner, with documentation updated on 2026-10-20. It is maintained with every change affecting users. Read the field tables alongside each workflow: a field can appear only after selecting a particular mode. Dates use your browser's local calendar and include both the start and end day.

<a id="quick-delivery-check"></a>

## A quick delivery check: people, leave, and surprises

**Delivery Studio helps you visualize team capacity and calculate how surprises affect delivery—in minutes.** It is not a project-management system or a tool for managing a Scrum workflow. Keep your tickets and daily work in Jira, ClickUp, Trello, or Linear; use this planner alongside them for a quick answer to **“If this changes, what happens to everything else?”** There is no automatic connection to those tools.

It is for delivery managers, Scrum masters, product owners, founders and co-founders, and team leads. Think of a five-minute planning conversation once the app is open and your inputs are ready. Setup starts with a feature timeline. Sprints are optional; leave **Plan in sprints** off to use working-day estimates without creating a sprint.

1. **Sketch the work and people.** After setup, add a feature, choose Main assignees, select Per person, and use Working days. For example, enter three days for Alex and seven for Sam. Use By person on Timeline to see their separate bookings. If they start together at full availability with no dependencies or competing work, Alex can finish their part earlier while Sam continues. Any gap is potential unbooked capacity in the entered plan, not measured employee inactivity.
2. **Include time off.** In Team & leave, add personal full/partial leave. Or create a reusable calendar template with leave/holiday periods and apply it to one person, several people, or all current app members. Creating the template alone does not affect dates. Personal leave follows people across apps; holidays apply within this app. Review the resulting absence bars and forecasts.
3. **Try a surprise.** On Timeline, enter What-if planning. Add a new urgent feature or raise an existing feature's priority. A new feature enters first in priority, so work sharing its people may move. Open Compare with saved plan to see affected features, old/new forecasts, and the calculated calendar-day differences. Changing work for someone with spare capacity may have a different effect from adding work for the busiest person.
4. **Decide before applying.** Apply experiment saves the experiment together; Discard experiment returns to the saved plan without applying the experimental changes. Outside What-if, Review scheduling changes appears before saving an edit that moves an existing forecast or changes allocated capacity; the impact panel also shows affected delivery dates. These are in-app explanations, not email or push alerts.
5. **Share the result.** Export timeline PDF or a feature-only PDF. Sprint capacity tables, accepted-date delay explanations, and sprint-plan exports are available as deeper workflows when useful.

Working days describe effort and availability. Calendar days describe the change between forecast dates. A three-day/seven-day feature is ten person-days of effort, but can finish in seven working days when the contributions run in parallel under the example's conditions. Use the actual schedule to evaluate leave, dependencies, allocation, and other bookings.

See the [README](../README.md) for an overview of Delivery Studio. The remaining sections explain every current field and workflow.

## Start, navigate, and save

For the portable download, extract the whole ZIP and open **Delivery Studio.app** on Mac or **Delivery Studio.exe** on Windows. It opens the planner in your browser on a local address. No Node installation or terminal is needed. See [Download and installation](download-and-install.md) for processor selection, first-open warnings, and recovery.

For the source edition on Mac, double-click **Start Delivery Studio.command**, keep its terminal open, and use `http://127.0.0.1:4311`. The old Start Sprint Studio.command is a compatibility alias. Closing the terminal stops this source edition; saved information remains on your computer. Source installation commands are in the README.

After an app update, the running local server must also restart to load new save rules. Finish saving or cancel any open forms first. Stop the planner in its running terminal with Control-C, launch **Start Delivery Studio.command** again, then refresh your browser. Refreshing alone only reloads the interface; the launcher also reuses an already running server. A server left on an older version can discard newly added settings when you save. If a new option disappears after saving, restart the updated app and reapply that option; do not refresh commitment dates to work around it. Saved records remain on disk, but refreshing clears unsaved form drafts and browser-session undo history.

The first visit to an empty workspace opens the setup wizard. After setup, **Current app** selects the app whose timeline, features, team, sprints, and settings you are viewing. **All apps overview** shows the workspace overview; **Add an app** opens another setup wizard. Navigation buttons open **Timeline**, **Features**, **Team & leave**, **Sprints & plans**, **App settings**, and **All apps overview**. Overview cards show the team/feature count and latest forecast; open a card to plan that app. Timeline opens directly to its planning controls and chart, without summary cards. Screens use their page heading and the Current app selector for context; there is no breadcrumb/Backup header bar.

The navigation starts expanded on each visit. After **three idle seconds** it becomes an icon rail. Pointer hover, keyboard focus inside navigation, and an open app picker pause this timer; leaving starts a fresh three seconds. **Expand navigation** reopens labels; **Collapse navigation** closes them immediately. Icons remain clickable with their names available to assistive technology and on hover. The Current app picker remains usable when collapsed. Tablets use the same vertical rail. Below 600px, expanded labels overlay the content; choosing a screen closes them to the icon rail. This navigation state is temporary, separate from saved timeline preferences.

Your edits save through each form's Save button. On first load/reload of older data, the planner also saves missing planning anchors once before showing the schedule; this metadata upgrade adds no normal Undo entry. Lists and forecasts may update while the save is pending; the Saved message confirms that the server returned every submitted setting and advanced the workspace revision once. An out-of-date server is blocked before sending changes; restart Delivery Studio and retry. If a returned save cannot be verified, reload to inspect the saved state before trying again. If saving fails, changes are rolled back. Read the error, keep/correct the draft when its form remains open, and retry. Field guidance remains visible when a field is invalid, so nearby controls stay in place while you correct it. A successful save closes an editor. **Cancel**, the dialog's **Close dialog** button, and Escape discard an unsaved editor. Dismissible notices use **Dismiss message**. Settings and plan text have their own saving rules below.

**Undo last change**, below Add an app in the navigation area, reverses up to 20 successful changes during the current browser session. It is available from every planner screen, including tablets, and disabled when there is no undo history or while a save is pending. It includes deletion, calendar application, commitment refresh, and backup restore. Reloading clears undo history. Undo itself saves the previous state; it can fail if another browser tab has changed the workspace. If you see “workspace changed in another tab,” export any draft text you need, reload, and reapply your intended change to the newest state. The app prevents overlapping saves. **Retry connection** reloads the app if opening the database fails.

## Close, reopen, and update the portable download

**Quit Delivery Studio** appears at the bottom right when you open the portable app's browser window. Save or cancel open editors and finish or discard a What-if experiment before clicking it. A confirmation warns that unsaved drafts and experiments will be lost. Cancel keeps the app running. Confirm requests shutdown; the interface checks that the local server became unreachable before showing **Delivery Studio stopped**; the planner underneath is disabled. If the server remains reachable or quitting fails, an error remains visible and you can retry. Closing a browser tab alone leaves the server running. Opening the application again reopens the existing instance; after quitting, opening it starts the saved workspace again.

Saved records live in `~/Library/Application Support/DeliveryStudio` on Mac, or `%LOCALAPPDATA%\DeliveryStudio` on Windows, outside the downloaded application folder. Quit before replacing the application folder with an update. Reopen the new version and check your saved app. The source edition uses its own `data/` folder; transfer between editions through **Export full backup** and **Restore backup**, checking the destination before restoring. Local storage does not automatically sync computers. Back up your workspace regularly.

If startup fails, read the dialog and the `delivery-studio.log` file in the saved-data folder. Re-extract the entire download if files are missing, and check that the processor version matches your computer. A launch already starting/stopping can ask you to try again; restart the computer if it remains stuck. Recovery never requires deleting your saved database.

## Stakeholder mode for screen sharing

Click the **Delivery Studio logo** at the top left of navigation **five times within three seconds** to toggle **Stakeholder mode**. A **white outline around the logo** indicates that it is active. Five more clicks turn it off. The logo remains visible in the collapsed icon rail. Keyboard users can focus the logo button and press Enter or Space five times; its pressed state announces whether the mode is active. Incomplete click sequences expire after three seconds.

While active, Timeline uses **By feature**, hides **By person**, and removes feature expansion buttons and all already-expanded task/assignee rows. Feature bars, dates, calendars and other timeline controls remain available. Turning the mode off restores the previous grouping and any expanded rows still held by the current Timeline screen. Existing navigation/reload behavior for expanded rows is unchanged.

This display setting applies across apps and screens in the current browser tab and survives refresh. Normal mode is the default; closing the tab ends its session. It is separate from saved per-app timeline preferences and workspace backups. If session storage is unavailable, toggling still works until refresh. Toggling does not save planning records, change capacity or forecasts, accept delivery dates, or alter plans. Other screens, filters, editors and PDF exports keep their existing behavior; choose **Export features PDF** or **Export compact features PDF** when you want feature-only exported pages.

<a id="save-review"></a>

## Review scheduling changes before saving

When a change moves an existing feature's forecast or changes its allocated capacity, **Review scheduling changes** opens before anything is written. Each entry shows the feature, app, assignees, previous/new forecast dates, and calendar-day difference. Changed allocated working days and reasons are shown when available. **Save changes** submits the pending edit; **Keep editing**, Close dialog, Escape or clicking outside cancels the review and keeps the original editor's values for correction. A cancellation message confirms nothing was saved. Edits without changed forecasts or allocated capacity save in one step. Parallel work can reduce booked working days while keeping delivery dates; the review shows those working days and retains the saved estimate. Reviewing does not accept new delivery dates or rewrite finalized plan text. Network failures or revision conflicts retain the editor draft for retry or recovery.

<a id="working-days"></a>

## Working days throughout

Effort, partial leave, available capacity, booked work, reassigned work, delay explanations, What-if comparisons and timeline/PDF summaries use **working days**. One working day means the person’s configured **Hours per day**; that setting remains in setup and Team because people can have different workday lengths. For example, half a day is half of that person’s daily capacity. Displayed amounts round to three decimal places; saved effort keeps its precision.

Combined feature totals sum person-days after converting each assignee separately. One day from Alex plus one day from Sam is two person-days, even if they work on the same date. Resource totals reflect allocation: ten available workdates at 50% allocation provide five full-person working days. Feature estimate inputs retain their existing allocation-aware meaning: five estimated days at 50% reserve five half-days. The saved effort stays fixed when calendars or allocation change; a changed daily-hours setting changes its displayed day equivalent without changing saved effort. Calendar-day delays and sprint calendar lengths are separate measures.

No existing effort, leave, saved draft or finalized plan text is rewritten just to change display units. Older backups still restore. Editing a task owner or personal-leave person preserves its saved effort/absence amount and recalculates the displayed days for the newly selected person. Check the amount before saving.

## Create an app: setup with optional sprints

1. **Your app:** enter the app name and choose a color. The optional **Use example team** action fills the fictional Northstar app name when blank, six editable fictional people, and neutral planning guidance; it is not required for other apps.
2. **Your people:** **Add person** for each team member. Choose a new person or an existing workspace person. Enter name, role, daily hours, allocation, and optional weekend override. Remove an unwanted draft row with its remove button. At least one person is required; an existing person cannot join the same app twice. Check that everyone is in the review before finishing.
3. **Working calendar:** select default weekend days and the planning start date. People without overrides follow these weekends. **Plan in sprints** starts off. Leave it off to continue directly to review with no sprint created.
4. **Ready to plan:** review app, people, and calendar, then choose **Open my planner** to save them together. **Back** returns to the calendar. **Continue** validates the current step. **Cancel setup**, where offered for an additional app, discards the draft. No app or member is saved until finishing succeeds.

If you enable **Plan in sprints**, a **Sprint rhythm** step appears between calendar and review: choose cadence and first sprint number, set calendar days for custom duration, and enter optional plan terms. Review then includes the proposed first sprint. Switching the option off and on preserves those draft choices. You can add sprints later through Sprints & plans; a timeline-only app retains the normal two-week default for future sprint suggestions.

<a id="leave"></a>

## Add and edit personal leave

1. Open **Team & leave → Add leave**.
2. Choose the person, start/end dates, full-day or partial-day leave, and optional note.
3. Choose **Save leave**. The entry appears in **Leave calendar**, sorted by start date; named absence bars appear on the timeline when their dates are in view.
4. To correct an entry, choose **Edit** beside it. **Edit time off** opens with all saved values. Change any field and save. This updates the same entry; it does not add a second leave.
5. **Cancel**, Close dialog, or Escape discard the draft. The trash button removes that specific leave; Undo can restore it.

| Field/control       | Purpose, defaults, and valid values                                                             | Effect                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Person taking leave | Required; current app members; new leave initially selects the first member                     | Personal leave affects this person in every app; changing person frees the original person and books the new person                                    |
| Leave starts        | Required valid date; today for new leave, saved date when editing                               | First included absence day                                                                                                                             |
| Leave ends          | Required valid date, on/after start; today for new leave                                        | Last included absence day, including a same-day leave                                                                                                  |
| Full-day leave      | On by default                                                                                   | Removes all the person's daily capacity on included working days                                                                                       |
| Days off per day    | Visible only when Full-day leave is off; 0.01–1 working day; switching to partial starts at 0.5 | Caps available hours at the lower of the app allocation allowance and the person’s daily hours minus leave; overlapping leave is capped at daily hours |
| Leave note          | Optional, up to 1,000 characters; initially blank                                               | Describes the absence in the list and timeline                                                                                                         |
| Edit                | Opens this entry's saved fields                                                                 | Changes the existing absence after Save leave succeeds                                                                                                 |
| Remove leave        | Trash action on the entry                                                                       | Removes this absence, recalculating forecasts                                                                                                          |

Leave dates are inclusive. Weekends already have no availability; including them in a leave does not remove additional working time. Overlapping absences never create negative capacity. For a developer working Sunday–Thursday, a full Sunday–Thursday leave removes five working days. A five-sprint feature has 50 working days of work; its forecast moves according to the missed working days and intervening weekends, rather than reducing its saved effort.

Editing only dates or a note preserves an older saved partial amount even if a later workday change puts it outside the new-entry range. New amounts or a changed person require 0.01–1 day; validation allows the tiny precision difference from storage conversion. Saving invalid dates or day amounts shows an error and leaves the editor open. An unsuccessful network save retains the entered draft for retry and restores the saved state. While saving, actions cannot submit the same change twice. Editing preserves the entry's ID and template origin information, if any. It does not change the source template. Template matching uses source entry, person, and original dates: if you move a template-created leave to another person or date range, applying the original template again can add the original absence back. Review the leave list before reapplying.

Editing leave updates future forecasts and sprint resource values across apps. It does not rewrite completed actual dates, accepted commitment dates, saved draft text, or finalized snapshots. Shortening leave can bring a forecast forward; extending it can put an accepted commitment at risk.

## App holidays

Choose **Add holiday**, enter its name and date, then choose whether it covers the whole app or selected members. Save with **Save holiday**; Cancel discards it. Holidays are listed under **App holidays**. Use the trash action to remove one and Undo to restore it. Existing individual holiday records have removal controls; reusable template entries can be edited separately.

| Field/control                        | Purpose and values                                            | Effect                                                             |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Holiday name                         | Required nonblank name, up to 250 characters; initially blank | List/timeline label                                                |
| Holiday date                         | Required valid date; initially today                          | One full absence day                                               |
| Whole app holiday                    | On by default                                                 | All app members follow the holiday                                 |
| Team members / person checkboxes     | Visible when whole-app is off; at least one member required   | Limits the holiday to these people in this app                     |

The selected-member picker initially selects all current app members; deselect people who should keep working. For example, 50% allocation gives 0.5 working day here per available date. Taking 0.25 day of personal leave leaves 0.75 day globally, so this app still has up to 0.5 day; taking 0.75 day of leave leaves only 0.25 day here. An app holiday does not block the same person in another app unless that other app has its own matching holiday. Personal leave does. Capacity never becomes negative when leave and holidays overlap.

## Reusable calendar templates

Templates are named collections of concrete dates. Creating one alone changes no forecasts. In **Team & leave → Reusable calendars**, choose **New calendar template**, choose **Add calendar entry** for extra entries, and save. Use **Apply** on the template, select members in the current app, then apply it. The resulting absences are independent saved copies. Apply can add newly selected people; repeated matching entries are not counted twice.

Older partial templates retain their original fixed amount and display its eight-hour **standard day** equivalent; editing that amount converts the entry to a person-relative fraction. Existing applied copies remain unchanged. Templates do not repeat annually or update applied copies automatically. Edit/remove applied personal leave in Leave calendar; remove individual holidays in App holidays. If you edit template dates, a later application can create records for the new dates while preserving old copies.

Feature creation and editing use a right-side panel on desktop and a bounded drawer on tablet. The timeline stays visible behind it; the editor keeps keyboard focus until closed. Title, main assignees, estimates and Feature blocked are shown first. ## Features, estimates, and delivery dates

Open **Features → Add feature**, or use Add feature on Timeline. Enter title, choose main assignees and estimates, and optionally add a breakdown. Save with **Save feature**. Click a feature name in Features to open its read-only **Feature overview**. Use the pencil or a timeline bar to edit, or choose **Edit feature** from the overview. **Search features** matches the feature's searchable text; clear it to restore the list. Priority arrows or dragging feature rows in Timeline change planning order. New features enter first and may delay existing work using the same people; the delivery impact panel explains old/new dates and affected people. Remove a feature through its editor's Delete action; confirm when asked. Removal cleans dependency links, and Undo restores it.

<a id="automatic-status"></a>

### Automatic status and stable scheduling dates

Features and optional subtasks change status automatically from their scheduled dates and your browser’s local date. **Feature blocked** is an explicit feature display override; task statuses remain automatic. There are no expected-unblock or actual-date inputs in either editor.

| Display     | Date rule                                                                                           |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Planned     | Before the scheduled start; also used when no complete scheduled interval is available              |
| In progress | From scheduled start through scheduled end, including both days                                     |
| Done        | After the scheduled end                                                                             |
| Blocked     | Feature blocked is checked and saved; takes precedence over all feature date statuses until cleared |

For example, work scheduled for October 12–14 is Planned on October 11, In progress on October 12, 13 and 14, and Done on October 15. A one-day task is In progress for that day. A feature uses the earliest start through the latest end of its work; each task uses its own interval, so a feature can be In progress while one task is Done and another is Planned. Person-view bars use that person’s own aggregate interval. A gap, weekend or leave day within an interval still counts as In progress; status describes the delivery window, not activity on each day. Unscheduled work stays Planned and retains its planning warning. Target and accepted-commitment warnings do not change the status of fully scheduled work. If only some work has dates, its warning remains visible alongside the available dates.

New features get a saved planning anchor on their creation day. Scheduling starts no earlier than that anchor, the app planning start, Start no earlier than or its assigned sprint start, then applies calendars, dependencies and shared-person priority. Existing features without an anchor receive one on first load/reload, and the planner saves it with revision protection and verifies the returned state before showing the schedule. This upgrade adds only missing planning metadata and no normal Undo entry; it preserves existing anchors, estimates, effort, accepted commitments, draft text and finalized snapshots. A conflicting save from another tab is reloaded before retrying, preserving that newer edit. If the upgrade cannot save, the schedule stays unopened and the error explains the problem; restart an outdated server or retry after resolving the error. Once anchors are saved, future loads make no migration save. Restoring an older backup is supported: missing anchors are filled before reviewing and saving its schedule. No anchor input is needed.

The anchor prevents the whole estimate from moving forward just because another day passes or the app reloads. Calendar, dependency, assignment, effort, priority and start-constraint edits still recalculate the schedule, and status follows the resulting dates. Completed actual intervals saved by older versions keep their historical dates; other preserved legacy manual status/unblock values do not constrain anchored work. Automatic completion does not overwrite saved effort or create actual-date records.

Status and current forecasts refresh at local midnight and when you focus the window or return to a visible tab. This makes no save or Undo entry and does not accept commitment dates or regenerate plan text. The Timeline status filter offers Planned, In progress, Blocked and Done. Blocked matches the explicitly saved feature flag; other choices match automatic dates for features without that flag. A saved Blocked filter is retained on reload.

<a id="feature-blocked"></a>

### Marking a feature blocked

In a new or existing feature, check **Feature blocked** after estimates (below Assigned sprint when present) and choose **Save feature**. It appears as **Blocked** with a red badge in Features and red bars in both Timeline views. The Blocked text and matching legend identify the state without relying on color. Optional task bars keep their own automatic statuses. Unscheduled blocked features still show their planning warning alongside Blocked.

The flag stays set across reloads and midnight/focus updates, including after the forecast end. Clear **Feature blocked** and save to restore the current Planned, In progress or Done status. Cancel, Close dialog and Escape discard an unsaved toggle; **Undo last change** reverses a saved toggle. Required title, assignee and effort validation still applies; a failed save retains the editor draft.

Blocking changes status and color only. It does not pause scheduling or free capacity, predict an unblock date, move forecasts/dependencies, change saved effort or accept new commitment dates. Draft plans and finalized sprint text remain unchanged. Timeline PDF exports show Blocked labels and red feature/person bars; narrow bars may clip visible text, with the full label in the preview tooltip. Older backups without this boolean flag remain unblocked, even if they contain a preserved legacy manual Blocked status. Backups, Undo and What-if changes carry the new flag through the existing save workflow.

<a id="ignore-leave"></a>

## Ignore leave for a feature

In **Add feature** or **Edit feature → Scheduling options**, check **Ignore leave**, then choose **Save feature**. It starts unchecked. Checked features schedule all main assignees and optional tasks using their normal daily capacity without subtracting full or partial personal leave, including leave applied from reusable calendars. Adding, editing or removing personal leave therefore does not directly move this feature's work. The setting also works with **Keep other delivery dates**.

For example, two working days starting Monday finish Tuesday even if the assignee has personal leave on Monday and Tuesday. Clearing Ignore leave and saving makes that leave apply again, so the feature instead starts Wednesday and finishes Thursday, assuming no other constraints.

Weekends, app holidays (including template holidays), allocation, assigned sprint, start bounds, priority, competing bookings and dependencies still apply. This is a planning assumption that someone can cover the work during leave, not a permanent date freeze: an upstream dependency or competing feature delayed by leave can still change this feature's dates. Each person retains a shared capacity ledger across apps; this option does not create an extra booking allowance for other features. Other unchecked features continue respecting leave. Resource tables still report the person's real calendar availability; checked work can be forecast during that absence.

Feature-level leave-impact shading and details are omitted for checked features. Personal leave records, person-row shading, calendar rows and PDF calendar shading remain visible as factual absence information. New generated sprint-plan text marks features that ignore personal leave. Existing saved drafts, finalized text, estimates and accepted delivery dates are preserved; forecast changes can still change commitment risk.

The setting is retained across reload, backups and What-if Apply; older backups without it remain unchecked. Scheduling options opens automatically when editing a checked feature. Cancel, Escape or closing discards unsaved changes; validation/save errors preserve the draft for correction or retry. It is disabled while saving. **Undo last change** restores the previous saved setting. The existing scheduling review appears when the change affects existing delivery dates or allocations, including this feature.

<a id="parallel-work"></a>

### Work that uses capacity without pushing other delivery dates

Choose **Keep other delivery dates** in the feature editor, then **Save feature**. It works with shared or per-person estimates and optional task breakdowns. The feature still needs assignees and valid effort; its checkbox does not bypass validation. Existing features open with their saved setting, and missing settings from older backups mean unchecked.

For example, Alex has a five-day feature ending Thursday. Add a checked one-day feature overlapping that window: the original delivery stays Thursday and the original saved estimate stays five days, but its actual allocation becomes four days. The new feature gets one working day. The feature list shows **4 working days allocated; 1 working day capacity reassigned**, and its timeline bar's hover/keyboard description explains that the delivery window is held. The estimate and allocation are intentionally different measurements: holding a date does not establish that all originally estimated work fits.

Ordinary work is forecast using its usual priority, calendars and shared-person limits. Checked work gets a parallel calendar window; overlapping daily capacity is allocated to checked work first, then ordinary work in priority order. Actual bookings across all work remain within each person's daily limit and each app's allocation. This can affect people shared across apps. Capacity is reassigned only on overlapping booked dates for the same person. If several checked features compete, priority determines allocation, and a checked feature can itself show reduced or even zero allocated capacity while retaining its window. The app displays that shortfall rather than counting the same capacity twice. A feature in the backlog can consume/reassign capacity as usual even if it is not assigned to a sprint.

Weekends, member overrides, leave (unless Ignore leave is checked for that feature), holidays, app allocation, start constraints, assigned-sprint starts and preserved completed actual dates retain their scheduling rules. Explicit task/feature dependencies still control ordering and can change dependent dates; this checkbox controls capacity contention, and does not cancel dependency requirements. Later calendar changes can move forecast windows normally. This option does not store a permanent date freeze or replace accepted commitment dates. Capacity reassignment by itself is displayed as information; commitment risk continues to compare forecast delivery with the accepted date.

Removing the checked feature or reducing its overlapping effort restores capacity automatically. Unchecking and saving puts that feature back into normal shared-capacity scheduling, so its work can again delay other work. **Cancel** or Escape leaves the saved setting/allocation untouched. Unsuccessful saves retain the checkbox and other draft values for retry; it is disabled while saving to prevent duplicate changes. Navigation **Undo last change** restores the previous saved setting and recalculates allocations. Reload and full backup/restore preserve the option. No other feature's saved estimate, commitment, finalized snapshot or draft wording is silently rewritten.

**Sprints & plans → Scope & capacity** shows a **Reassigned** column only when capacity assigned to that sprint has been taken by parallel work within the selected sprint dates. **Remaining** keeps the original effort demand; **Fits here** sums actual allocated working days. Newly generated plan text identifies checked features and reassigned working days. Regenerate/save explicitly to update plan wording. Timeline PDF feature/person labels include allocated/reassigned working days where relevant, and mark checked feature rows **Parallel work**; the PDF still includes both groupings and no separate absence rows.

<a id="commitments"></a>

## Commitments, risk, and delivery impacts

A target is your desired date. A forecast is the calculated finish. An accepted commitment is the end of the sprint containing the forecast you explicitly agreed to.

Check **Accept delivery date** in a feature or **Committed** in its sprint. The first successful save captures the end of its delivery sprint. For example, a Wednesday forecast is accepted at that sprint’s ending date. The forecast itself stays Wednesday. Accepting a new commitment requires scheduled unfinished work; an unscheduled or automatically completed feature cannot establish a new accepted date. A multi-sprint feature is expected to cross sprint boundaries: that alone is not at risk. A committed unfinished feature becomes at risk when its forecast exceeds the accepted date, or becomes unscheduled. Displays show accepted date, current forecast, and delay in calendar days. Target warnings are separate comparisons.

Use **Refresh commitment date** on one feature, **Refresh app commitment dates**, or **Refresh sprint commitment dates** to accept the ends of the current delivery sprints as the new baseline. Bulk refresh includes unfinished committed features in that app/sprint. If one is unscheduled, the refresh reports which feature prevents acceptance and saves none of that bulk change. Legacy checkbox-only commitments show **Accept commitment dates** until explicitly refreshed. Unchecking and saving removes its accepted baseline. Turning the checkbox off/on in an unsaved edit retains its existing baseline.

Explicit saved sprint dates and overrides take precedence for delivery acceptance. Beyond generated sprints the app projects the app cadence and common weekend rules without creating sprint records. A forecast in a gap before a saved sprint uses that next delivery opportunity. Moving within the accepted sprint stays on track; finishing beyond its accepted ending becomes at risk. An owner-authorized one-time alignment moves existing accepted dates to the end of their original sprint, preserving acceptance timestamps and finalized plans; it never accepts a later forecast. Later calendar and priority changes do not silently accept new dates. Refreshing baselines does not rewrite saved plans: regenerate a draft if you want its wording to reflect new dates, then save/finalize explicitly. The delivery impact panel compares the previous and current forecasts and shows affected features, old/new dates, delay, and the feature/shared people causing an impact where applicable. Dismiss its notice when reviewed.

Scroll the page until the chart reaches the top of the browser to use that space, then scroll through rows inside the chart. Its maximum height updates automatically when you resize the window or rotate a tablet. Short timelines shrink to their content rather than adding empty rows. ### Leave shading in feature view

**By feature** automatically shades personal leave inside each unfinished assignee’s scheduled work window. **Partial leave impact** uses peach stripes when calendar capacity remains for at least one active assignee; **All unavailable** uses a stronger peach fill when every active assignee has zero calendar capacity. An assignee’s weekend or holiday can contribute to full unavailability when another assignee is on leave. Status colors on feature bars stay unchanged.

For example, Alex’s full-day leave while Sam is available produces stripes. If both are unavailable, the fill is stronger. A 0.25-day leave shows 0.75 working day remaining, subject to app allocation. Shading covers every saved leave date inside the active assignee’s work window, including overlapping weekends and holidays, so a continuous leave period has no blank gaps. Partial leave also remains visible when it does not reduce the app’s allocation. These backgrounds show calendar context; they do not imply extra capacity loss on already unavailable days. Weekends/holidays alone retain their existing backgrounds. Leave outside the visible or individual work window, completed work, unrelated people and unapplied templates do not produce feature leave shading. Expanding tasks retains their existing personal calendars. Filtering by person selects features; their parent rows still describe all active assignees.

Hover a shaded cell for the date, names, saved leave dates, notes, full-day or days-off-per-day setting, overlapping weekends/holidays, and remaining calendar capacity. For touch or keyboard access, choose the calendar button **Leave details** beside the feature name (announced as **Leave details for Feature**). It appears only when that feature has leave dates in the visible window. The **Feature leave details** dialog groups consecutive matching days and lists every active assignee, including available people and relevant weekends/holidays. It reports capacity reduced by leave only when there is an actual reduction; overlapping weekends and holidays add no extra loss. Saved leave dates are inclusive and may extend beyond the displayed range. The **Feature leave dates and people** region can be focused and scrolled with the keyboard.

Use Escape, **Close dialog**, or the backdrop to close and return to the calendar button. The dialog is read-only: there are no required fields, defaults to change, Save action or undo step. Manage leave from Team & leave. Collapsing the separate absence rows retains feature shading. What-if drafts recalculate the same backgrounds; Discard restores saved-calendar shading. This screen display does not change estimates, accepted dates, plans, exports or records. Calendar capacity is availability before competing work, not a promise of free capacity; a shaded day is not automatically one calendar day of delivery delay. Use **Why delayed?** for commitment explanations. PDF export retains its existing calendar display.

Grouping, Day/Week/Month zoom, Timeline range, start date, collapsed leave state, and the four filters are remembered **per app in this browser**. Defaults for a new app view are By feature, Month, two months, today, expanded time off and all filters. Reopening the app restores the last view. Invalid or unavailable browser storage falls back safely; these preferences are outside workspace backups and never alter capacity or forecasts.

1. Select the app, start date (Today or Previous week / Next week) and **Timeline range** of 1–6 months. The export uses the entire selected window, including dates hidden by horizontal scrolling.
2. Choose **Export compact features PDF** for a vertically compacted feature-only timeline: the **Feature** column contains titles only, without date or effort subtitles. Short titles use shorter rows; long titles wrap and receive enough height to remain readable. Timeline bars, date headers, calendar shading, Blocked indicators and unscheduled warnings remain available. Choose **Export features PDF** for the standard feature-only layout with forecast subtitles and regular row spacing, with no **By person** pages. Choose **Export timeline PDF** to include **By feature** followed by **By person**. All three buttons use the selected date window regardless of the grouping or filters currently selected on screen. A new local preview opens and starts the browser print dialog. Feature rows show the overall forecast under a left column called **Feature**; person rows show that person's assigned features and dates. Optional task breakdown rows and separate leave/holiday rows are omitted.
3. Choose **Save as PDF** as the print destination. All layouts request **A4**, **landscape**, with 8mm margins. The narrower label column gives more width to the timeline; long titles wrap within it. Keep background graphics enabled to retain calendar shading and turn off browser-added headers/footers if the dialog offers them. Choose the PDF filename and save location in the browser dialog.
4. Cancel the print dialog to return to the preview without saving a file. Choose **Print / Save PDF** there to try again, or **Close preview** to close it. If pop-ups are blocked, the Timeline shows instructions to allow them for the local planner; then choose the same export button again. If printing cannot start automatically, use the preview's retry button.

A person with more simultaneous bars than fit on a page continues on another page with the same name marked **Continued**; features are not duplicated within that person's date section. The printed legend explains the colors; detailed absence names and day amounts remain in Team & leave.

It makes no network requests or saved-data changes, does not accept new commitments or finalize plans, and preserves the current screen's grouping/collapse state. No undo is needed after exporting or cancelling. If you later change features or availability, export again for an updated PDF. An empty app exports one labeled empty-state page per selected grouping in each date section. This is the timeline export; the separate **Print / PDF** control in Sprints & plans prints plan text instead.

## Sprint calendars and feature assignment

Open **Sprints & plans**. Select a sprint card; **New sprint** creates another, and **Edit sprint** changes the selected sprint. Suggested dates use the app cadence and skip weekends shared by every app member, taking personal overrides into account. With Saturday as the only shared weekend, suggested sprints start Sunday and end Friday. These calendar dates are independent of a ten-working-day estimated sprint.

| Field/control                     | Purpose/default/values                                                                                     | Effect                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sprint number                     | Whole number 1–99999; latest chronological sprint number plus one suggested; a duplicate must be corrected | Must be unique within this app                                                                                |
| Sprint starts / Sprint ends       | Required inclusive valid dates, end on/after start; suggested by cadence                                   | Explicit overrides take precedence; overlaps with another sprint are rejected by the editor                   |
| Apply app’s default duration      | Uses app cadence and shared weekends                                                                       | Recalculates both proposed start and end, skipping shared weekends; saving accepts them                       |
| Planning notes                    | Optional text, up to 10,000 characters; initially blank                                                    | Notes in the generated plan                                                                                   |
| Save sprint / Cancel              | Saves/discards the calendar draft                                                                          | Changes forecasts for work assigned to that starting sprint                                                   |
| Assigned sprint in feature editor | Choose this sprint from a feature’s editor                                                                 | Assigns its starting sprint; there is no separate assignment picker in this workbench                         |
| Remove feature from sprint        | Action on assigned row                                                                                     | Clears assignment and explicit commitment; retains the feature                                                |
| Committed                         | Checkbox on assigned feature; default follows feature                                                      | Accepts/removes its delivery baseline as described above                                                      |
| Delete sprint                     | Confirmation required                                                                                      | Removes sprint and its saved draft/snapshot; features remain but lose this assignment and explicit commitment |

Removing a feature from a sprint or deleting its sprint also clears its accepted commitment baseline. Assign and explicitly commit it again when appropriate. Changing default cadence does not silently rewrite existing sprint dates. Each sprint can be overridden independently. Calendar-month cadence follows monthly anniversaries clamped for shorter months; a January 31 start ends February 27. Multi-sprint work may finish well beyond the assigned sprint. A sprint's At risk indicator reflects delayed accepted commitments, not merely long features.

**Sprint goal** is automatic and read-only in the workbench. Features forecast during this sprint appear in priority order, including continuing work assigned to an earlier starting sprint and scheduled backlog work. A feature finishing in this sprint uses its name alone. Work finishing later reads **Start [feature]** when its forecast starts in this sprint, or **Proceed with [feature]** when it started earlier. For example, a 25-working-day feature over three two-week sprints reads Start Checkout — Phase 1, Proceed with Checkout — Phase 1, then Checkout — Phase 1. Leave or competing work can change these stages when the forecast changes. Assigned work without a scheduled start is marked **(not scheduled)**; a missing finish is never presented as a normal finishing goal. An empty workbench says No features scheduled in this sprint. The plan's goal section stays blank for an empty sprint.

The sprint editor no longer asks for a separate goal. Use **Planning notes** for extra context, or edit the generated Markdown before saving. Older saved goal values remain in backups for compatibility but do not override automatic goals. Existing saved drafts and finalized plans keep their text until explicitly regenerated or replaced.

## Resource allocation

The selected sprint's resource table is calculated after calendars and leave. It is not directly editable.

| Column     | Meaning                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Person     | Member’s name; role is shown elsewhere in Team and plan objectives                                                                              |
| Available  | Working-day capacity during the sprint after app/member weekends, leave, holidays, and allocation                                               |
| Remaining  | Remaining demand for this person's work assigned to the sprint; it can exceed Available for multi-sprint work                                   |
| Fits here  | Forecast working days from that demand scheduled inside these sprint dates                                                                      |
| Other apps | Working days actually allocated for this shared person on other apps inside these dates                                                         |
| Reassigned | Conditional: capacity removed from work assigned to this sprint on dates inside it, to accommodate parallel work while holding delivery windows |

Adjust inputs through Team & leave, feature estimates/tasks, priorities, or sprint dates. A person cannot be booked beyond their global daily hours. Demand and available capacity are different measurements; a large Remaining amount is expected for a feature estimated across several sprints.

## Generate, edit, save, and export sprint plans

1. Select a sprint and review its goal, assigned features, explicit commitments, and resources.
2. Choose **Generate sprint plan** (or **Regenerate draft** when a draft already exists). The planner creates Markdown with the existing sprint-plan headings: sprint goal, objectives by scope, resource allocation, completion conditions, and general terms, plus commitment context. **Resources Allocated** lists all current app team members, including those without feature assignments. **Capacity and Availability** lists each person’s overlapping leave dates, full-day or partial-day amount and note, plus holiday names, dates and affected people (or whole app). Dates are clipped to the selected sprint. There is no hour-based resource ledger in the generated document. For example: “Alex is on leave from Jan 8, 2030 – Jan 9, 2030: 0.5 working day off per day (Appointment).” The Scope & capacity tab retains the separate working-day totals for planning.
3. Choose the **Sprint plan** tab (the other tab is **Scope & capacity**), then **Edit text** / **Preview** to review **Sprint plan Markdown** and its formatted preview. The field accepts up to 100,000 characters when saved; it starts from the existing saved draft/snapshot or the generated content. Your text edits change the document, not scheduled work.
4. **Save draft** stores the current wording for future visits. Leaving/reloading before saving can discard editor changes. Regeneration replaces the draft editor with freshly calculated wording; save it explicitly.
5. **Finalize plan** (or **Save new snapshot** for an already finalized sprint) saves the exact current text as the sprint snapshot. Later feature, calendar, or commitment changes do not rewrite it. Finalizing clears the saved draft. **Saved plan** loads the previous finalized snapshot into the preview. Explicitly choose Save new snapshot if you want to replace it.
6. **Export Markdown** exports plan text as a `.md` file. **Print / PDF** opens browser printing for the current plan text, including unsaved text in Edit text mode; choose Save as PDF there. Exporting does not itself save draft edits or finalize a snapshot.

The generated **Sprint Goal** uses the automatic feature list described above, with Start/Proceed with only for unfinished multi-sprint work. Features with forecast windows overlapping the sprint are included even if assigned to an earlier sprint or still in the backlog. Features explicitly assigned to this sprint remain in commitment context even if unscheduled or forecast to start later; they are not silently described as finishing here.

**Objectives by Scope** comes only from the feature's saved optional subtasks, never from its main assignees, team roles, or estimates. **Web Frontend & Full stack**, **Backend**, and **Mobile** headings remain blank if there are no matching subtasks. FE/Frontend and BE/Backend spellings are grouped under their standard headings; additional entered scopes such as Design/QA appear only when explicit tasks use them. Each item contains the feature name and task title, and appears only in sprints overlapping that task's forecast window. Unscheduled tasks explicitly assigned to the sprint remain visible; the commitment section states their scheduling problem. A feature without a breakdown still appears in the goal and commitment context, with no invented scope detail.

A finalized sprint card is marked Finalized; risk can still change when its accepted commitments become delayed. Saved text remains historical. Review resources and regenerate/finalize explicitly to publish a new local snapshot.

<a id="backups"></a>

## Backups, restore, and local data

**Export full backup** in **App settings** downloads JSON for the ENTIRE workspace: all apps, people, memberships, features/tasks, estimates, commitments, personal leave, holidays/templates, sprints, draft text, and finalized snapshots. Export regularly before major edits. A backup is not limited to the current app.

**Restore backup** is a file field accepting JSON. Choose a file up to 15 MB, confirm replacement, and wait for successful saving. Restore validates the entire file and references before replacing the saved workspace. Invalid JSON, unsupported versions, invalid dates, missing references, and duplicate records are rejected without replacing saved data. Canceling confirmation keeps the workspace unchanged. Undo can reverse a successful restore during the current session. Restored commitment baselines and saved plan text are preserved as provided by the backup.

Your database is `data/planner.sqlite`, with companion WAL/SHM files while running. Do not edit the database manually while the planner is running. Keep backups on your own computer. No subscription, hosted database, remote font, AI API, or paid UI kit is needed. Your database, backups, credentials, and generated reports stay on your computer. A fresh source download starts with an empty workspace.

## Temporary What-if planning

1. Finish or cancel any open form, go to **Timeline**, and choose **What-if planning**. This starts an experiment from the saved workspace across **all apps**. The banner identifies the temporary mode; no named scenario is created.
2. Use the same feature, leave, holiday/template, team, sprint and app-settings editors. Their Save buttons update the experiment only. Notices say **Updated in your experiment**. Switching apps keeps the experiment active; shared people and personal leave affect the other apps' experimental forecasts too.
3. Open **Compare with saved plan** in the banner. It lists added, removed or changed features, app and affected assignees, saved/experimental finish dates, calendar-day differences, and actual allocated working days. The comparison also shows changes caused only by a different full-workday length, even when saved effort and finish dates remain the same. The person totals cover all scheduled work across the full forecast, rather than one sprint. A date can stay fixed while allocation drops because of Keep other delivery dates. Calendar/settings edits with no visible feature change are still included in Apply.
4. **Undo last change** reverses the most recent experiment edit, with up to 20 steps in memory. It makes no database write. **Apply experiment** becomes enabled when the workspace differs from its starting state; it saves all experiment changes together as one revision and one normal Undo step. The comparison is your pre-save review in this mode; there is no second scheduling-review dialog for individual experimental edits.
5. **Discard experiment** saves no experimental edits. It reloads the latest saved workspace, including changes made in another tab. If those saved records come from an older version and lack planning anchors, reload performs the metadata upgrade described above. If reloading fails, the experiment remains available and an error explains the problem. A newer external revision clears outdated normal Undo history.

## Worked examples and common questions

- **Checkout – Phase 1, five sprints for all team:** select every main assignee, choose Same for everyone, Sprints, and 5. This reserves 50 working days for each. A new higher-priority feature using one of those people may delay the overall finish; review Delivery impacts.
- **Different developer/designer amounts:** choose Per person, give a developer 1 sprint and a designer 3 Working days. Each person's capacity/calendar drives their finish; the feature ends when both finish.
- **Correct Alex Morgan's leave:** choose Edit on his entry, adjust dates/day amount/note, and Save leave. Confirm the same entry is updated, then view the timeline and sprint resources. Undo restores the original saved entry if needed.
- **A five-sprint commitment crosses a two-week sprint:** this is expected. It becomes at risk when it moves beyond its ACCEPTED finish, not merely the sprint end. Refresh commitment dates explicitly accepts the new forecast.
- **A saved plan does not show the latest dates:** saved text is a snapshot. Regenerate draft, review, save, then finalize again if appropriate.
- **A person seems missing:** clear Search team, select the correct app, and check membership. People from another app require linking through Add person.
- **Status has not changed:** check the scheduled start/end and your local date. Start and end days are In progress; Done begins the next day. Return focus to the tab to refresh the date. Unscheduled work stays Planned until its planning problem is resolved.
- **No forecast:** check dependency cycles or unscheduled dependencies, assignments, archived app state, and available calendars. Work without dates remains Planned with its warning. Correct the planning problem before accepting commitments.
